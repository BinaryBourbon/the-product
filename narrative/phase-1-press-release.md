# Tidepool Replaces the IDE as the Primary Surface for Engineering and Product Work

**The working environment where engineers and product managers plan, execute, and follow up on work — end to end, without switching tools.**

[CITY] — Tidepool today announced the general availability of Tidepool, a workspace built to replace the IDE as the primary surface where engineering and product teams spend their working day. Unlike tools that sit alongside existing workflows, Tidepool is where work happens: teams plan what to build, execute on it, and follow up on outcomes — all from a single working environment.

Every transition cost me ten minutes of re-orientation. Tidepool is where my work actually lives now. ---

Tidepool is organized around the three phases that define every piece of engineering and product work. In the **Plan** phase, teams scope features, align on requirements, and surface the context — past decisions, related changes, open questions — needed to build the right thing. In the **Execute** phase, engineers write and ship code while PMs drive decisions across tickets and systems, all within the same workspace. In the **Follow-up** phase, both engineers and PMs close the loop: confirming what shipped, measuring whether it worked, and identifying what to address next.

The workspace connects the data that already links these phases together. Commits, deploys, product events, and performance traces share identifiers and timestamps — but today every tool treats them in isolation, forcing manual cross-tool detective work to get a complete picture. Tidepool exposes those connections natively. When an engineer spots an anomaly mid-execution, the path from a PostHog event to the GitHub commit that caused it to the Honeycomb trace that explains it is one step, not a sequence of copy-paste queries across tabs. This capability serves the execute and follow-up phases; it is not a feature users visit occasionally, but part of how the workspace keeps plan, execution, and outcome in continuous alignment.
